Pipeline

From raw data to optimal hubs

01
Ingest real demand

WorldPop 2020 raster disaggregated into 41,288 population-weighted demand points. Your own order or shipment data maps to the same coordinate system.

02
Apply constraints

Feasible set restricted to C+CDA OZP commercial polygons. Competitor exclusion zones, MTR proximity, and custom no-go areas are handled as KKT inequality constraints.

03
Solve and compare

Weiszfeld iteration and Lloyd's algorithm find the k-median optimum. Road-network distance replaces Euclidean for operationally realistic results. Every result ships as an interactive Folium map.

Mathematics

Operations research that holds up

Not a heuristic, not a black box. Every result is reproducible, auditable, and grounded in facility-location theory.

  • Weber problem solved with gradient descent, Newton, BFGS, and Weiszfeld iteration — all four converge to the same point
  • KKT conditions enforce constraint feasibility: commercial zoning, exclusion zones, corridor restrictions
  • Uncapacitated k-median via Lloyd's algorithm with multi-start restarts to avoid local minima
  • OZP commercial polygon constraint: 590 zones filtered from 11,963 ArcGIS polygons, unioned to 499-piece MultiPolygon
  • Road-network distance via OSMnx HK driving graph (18,820 nodes, 35,848 edges) — 2.33 km Euclidean shift at k=1
  • k-sweep over k ∈ {3, 5, 8, 10, 15, 20} quantifies exactly where marginal returns disappear

Weber optimum (single hub)

Minimise population-weighted sum of distances to a single facility. Constrained to OZP commercial land.

Optimum: Sham Shui Po (114.17°E, 22.34°N) — all four solvers agree

k-median (k hubs, road network)

Lloyd's algorithm with Weiszfeld updates. Road-network distance replaces Euclidean for driving-time realism.

k=5: avg 5,852 m/resident — 54.4% reduction from single-hub baseline
